1.Getting started

What you need, and the first two minutes with the app.

What it is for

CCTV Wall Pro shows live video from IP security cameras on your Fire TV, Fire tablet or Android device. It talks to your cameras directly over your own network: there is no cloud service and no account.

Step by step

  1. Make sure your cameras and this device are on the same home or office network (Wi-Fi or Ethernet).
  2. Open CCTV Wall Pro. The welcome screen explains the app in three lines.
  3. Choose See the demo wall to try the wall with four built-in demo clips, or Add my camera to add your own straight away.
  4. Add your first camera (chapter 3). The demo clips are removed by themselves when you do.

What you should see

After the welcome screen the app opens on the camera wall. From then on it always opens on the wall you used last.

Common problems

My camera is a Ring, Blink, Google Nest, Arlo or most Wyze models.These cameras only send video to their maker's cloud and offer no RTSP or ONVIF, so no viewer app can show them. Cameras and recorders that offer RTSP or ONVIF work: Hikvision, Dahua, Amcrest, Lorex, Reolink, TP-Link Tapo, EZVIZ, Uniview, Axis, Foscam and many more.
Can I watch my cameras when I am away from home?The app connects inside your own network. It can reach a camera elsewhere only if you set up a VPN to that network yourself.

2.Application overview

The Home screen, the side menu and where everything lives.

What it is for

Home is the dashboard: it shows how many cameras you have and whether any need attention, and opens the wall, groups, saved layouts and quick actions.

Step by step

  1. Press Back on the wall to reach Home.
  2. On a TV or tablet, the menu on the left has Home, Wall, Cameras, Groups, Help and Settings. Move left with the remote to reach it.
  3. On a phone, Home shows big buttons for the same places, and every screen has a back arrow at the top.
  4. Open wall starts monitoring; Rotate cameras shows each camera full screen in turn; Add camera starts the setup.

What you should see

The line under the title reads, for example, "6 cameras · all good at last check", or says how many need attention.

Common problems

The remote does nothing on a screen.Every button can be reached with the arrow keys; the one you are on has a bright blue ring. If no ring is visible, press an arrow key once.

3.Adding IP cameras

The four ways to add a camera, and which one to pick.

What it is for

Cameras differ: some answer an ONVIF search, others only give an RTSP address. The Add a camera screen offers every way in.

Step by step

  1. Open Home and choose Add camera (or Cameras > Add).
  2. Find ONVIF cameras: searches your network (chapter 5). Best for Hikvision, Dahua, Reolink, Axis, Uniview and most recorders.
  3. Add from your phone: scan the QR code and type the details on your phone instead of with the remote.
  4. Choose the brand and type the IP: the stream path is filled in for you.
  5. Paste an RTSP address: for anything that gives you an rtsp:// address (chapter 4).
  6. Every way ends with a live test (chapter 7) before the camera is saved.

What you should see

The new camera appears on the wall in the first free tile, and in Cameras.

Common problems

Add from your phone says the TV did not answer.Keep the Add from your phone screen open on the TV while you fill in the form, and make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi, not on mobile data or a guest network.
I don't know my camera's IP address.Look in the camera's own app (device info or network settings), or in your router's list of connected devices. Giving the camera a fixed (reserved) address in your router stops it changing later.

4.Manual RTSP setup

Adding a camera by brand and IP address, or with a full rtsp:// address.

What it is for

RTSP is the standard way cameras send live video. If you know the camera's IP address or its RTSP address, you can add it by hand.

Step by step

  1. Choose Add camera > Choose the brand and type the IP (or Paste an RTSP address).
  2. Type a name, such as Front door, and if you like a short description.
  3. By brand: pick the brand, type the IP address, and change the port only if your camera uses another one. The main and sub stream paths are filled in; read the tip under the brands.
  4. Full address: paste the main stream address, for example rtsp://192.168.1.20:554/stream1, and optionally a sub stream address for the wall.
  5. Type the camera's user name and password under Login.
  6. Choose Test and save.

What you should see

The test shows the live picture with its format, for example H.264 · 1920×1080, and a Save camera button.

Common problems

The address contains the login, like rtsp://admin:pass@…That works. When you save, the login is taken out of the address and stored encrypted, and the address is saved without it.
"The address must start with rtsp://"Only RTSP addresses are accepted here. HTTP snapshot or web page addresses are not video streams.
The picture stays grey or freezes after a few seconds.Turn on Use TCP only. Some routers and Wi-Fi extenders drop UDP video.

5.ONVIF discovery

Letting the app find ONVIF cameras on your network.

What it is for

ONVIF is a standard most security cameras and recorders speak. With it the app finds the camera, reads its video profiles and stream addresses, and can control pan/tilt/zoom.

Step by step

  1. Choose Add camera > Find ONVIF cameras. The search runs for about five seconds.
  2. Pick your camera from the list. Its name and model are shown when the camera reports them.
  3. Type the camera's user name and password and choose Connect.
  4. Check the camera name, the full-screen (main) stream and the wall (sub) stream. The app picks the largest picture for full screen and a smaller one for the wall.
  5. Choose Test and save.

What you should see

After Connect you see the maker, model and firmware, and a list of profiles such as MainStream · 1920×1080 · H.264.

Common problems

No camera answered.Turn on ONVIF in the camera's app or web page (many cameras ship with it off, and some need a separate ONVIF user). Make sure the device and camera are on the same network, and turn off "AP isolation" or "client isolation" on the router. You can also type the camera's IP under Know the camera's IP? and choose Connect.
The camera refused this login.Use the camera's admin user, or the ONVIF user made in its app. ONVIF logins also fail when the camera's clock is far off; the app uses the camera's own clock, but a camera with no time set at all may still refuse.

6.Camera credentials

Which login to use, and how the app keeps it safe.

What it is for

Most cameras need a user name and password before they send video. CCTV Wall Pro stores each login encrypted on this device.

Step by step

  1. Use the login of the camera itself, not the cloud account of its app. Tapo: make a Camera Account in the Tapo app (Camera settings > Advanced settings). EZVIZ: user admin, and the 6-letter verification code on the camera's label.
  2. Type it in the Login fields when you add or edit a camera. Show reveals the password while you type.
  3. To change a login later, open the camera, choose Edit, type the new login and save.

What you should see

Camera details shows Login: Stored encrypted on this device.

Common problems

Wrong user name or password, after it worked before.The camera's password may have been changed in its app. Edit the camera and type the new one. The app stops trying after three refusals so the camera does not lock its own account.
Where is my password kept?Encrypted with a key from the Android Keystore that never leaves the device. It is not in backups, not in logs, and not in problem reports. Deleting a camera deletes its login.

7.Testing connections

The live test before saving, and testing a saved camera.

What it is for

The test plays the camera once, right away, and tells you in plain words what happened, so you know it works before you rely on it.

Step by step

  1. When adding: choose Test and save. A window opens and connects to the camera.
  2. If the picture appears, the line under it says It works and shows the video format. Choose Save camera.
  3. If it fails, the window says why and what to do. Choose Try again after fixing it, Back to the form to change details, or Save anyway for a camera that is switched off right now.
  4. For a saved camera: open Cameras, pick the camera and choose Test connection. If the camera has a sub stream you can test it too.

What you should see

A live picture within a few seconds; the test gives up with a reason after 15 seconds without a picture.

Common problems

The test says The camera can't be reached.The camera is off, on another network, or its IP address changed. Network diagnostics (chapter 19) checks whether its port answers.
The test says The camera has no stream at this address.The camera answered but not at this path. Pick the brand again, or try the other stream (main or sub).

8.Camera groups

Watching only the cameras of one place: Home Security, Office, Shop, Warehouse, Reception.

What it is for

A group is a set of cameras you watch together. Opening a group shows only its cameras on the wall, and it can open with its own saved layout.

Step by step

  1. Open Groups and choose New group.
  2. Type a name or pick one: Home Security, Office, Shop, Warehouse, Reception, Outdoor, Night.
  3. Tick the cameras that belong to the group. A camera can be in several groups.
  4. Under Opens with, choose The smallest wall that fits, or one of your saved layouts.
  5. Choose Save and open. Next time, open the group from Home or Groups.

What you should see

The wall's title shows the group's name instead of All cameras.

Common problems

How do I see all cameras again?Open Groups and choose Show all cameras on the wall.
Does deleting a group delete its cameras?No. Only the group is deleted.

One camera filling the wall.

The classic 2 × 2 wall.

What it is for

13.Nine camera layout

A 3 × 3 wall for larger systems.

What it is for

Nine cameras at once for shops, warehouses and offices, on devices strong enough to decode them.

Step by step

  1. On the wall, choose the button with nine squares.
  2. Check Settings > Cameras playing at once: it shows how many this device plays smoothly. Tiles over that number show Paused until opened.
  3. For best results give each camera a sub stream (most cameras have one) and keep Use sub streams on the wall turned on.

What you should see

Up to nine live tiles; on smaller devices the first ones play and the rest wait.

Common problems

Why not all nine on my Fire TV Stick?Small streaming sticks have a limited number of hardware video decoders. The app plays as many as the device can handle smoothly instead of letting every tile stutter. You can change the number in Settings at your own risk.

14.Fullscreen mode

One camera on the whole screen, with sound and controls.

What it is for

Full screen uses the camera's main (highest quality) stream for a closer look.

Step by step

  1. On the wall, select a camera and press OK (or tap it).
  2. Left and Right show the previous and next camera.
  3. Press OK to show the controls: Previous, Next, Rotate, PTZ (on cameras that support it), Sound, Reconnect and Details. They hide after five seconds.
  4. Press Back (or the back arrow) to return to the wall.

What you should see

The camera's name, address and video format at the bottom, and its status at the top-left.

Common problems

There is no sound.Full screen starts muted. Choose Muted to turn sound on. Only cameras with a microphone send sound.
The picture shows a problem and Trying again.The app reconnects by itself, waiting a little longer each time. Choose Reconnect now to try at once.

15.Automatic camera rotation

Showing each camera full screen in turn.

What it is for

Rotation turns a spare TV into a monitor that cycles through your cameras by itself.

Step by step

  1. Press Play/Pause on the remote on the wall, or choose Rotate on the wall or on Home.
  2. Each camera shows for the time set in Settings > Rotation (5 to 60 seconds). The top-left says ROTATING, the interval and the position, for example 2/6.
  3. Left and Right move to the previous or next camera at once. Play/Pause pauses and restarts rotation.
  4. Choose which cameras take part in Settings > Cameras in the rotation (none ticked = all, or the open group's).
  5. To start rotating every time the app opens, turn on Start rotating when the app opens.
  6. Press Back to stop rotation immediately.

What you should see

A camera that is off or unreachable is skipped after a short look, so the rotation never stops on a blank screen.

Common problems

Rotation skips a camera.That camera could not be reached. Open Diagnostics to see why.

16.PTZ controls

Pan, tilt and zoom for cameras that move.

What it is for

Pan/tilt/zoom cameras can be turned and zoomed from the app over ONVIF. The PTZ button only appears for cameras added through ONVIF whose profile reports PTZ support.

Step by step

  1. Open the camera full screen and press OK to show the controls.
  2. Choose PTZ. A panel with arrows and zoom buttons opens on the right.
  3. Hold an arrow (hold OK on the remote, or keep your finger on it) to move; the camera stops when you let go.
  4. Use Zoom in and Zoom out the same way.
  5. Choose a preset name to jump to a saved position. Choose Save position to store the current view as a new preset.
  6. Choose Close or press Back to hide the panel.

What you should see

The camera moves while the button is held and stops when it is released.

Common problems

There is no PTZ button.The camera was added by RTSP address, or its ONVIF profile has no PTZ. Add it again through Find ONVIF cameras.
The camera refuses to move.Some cameras need a login with operator or admin rights for PTZ. The message under the panel shows what the camera answered.

19.Network requirements

What your network needs for smooth video, and how to check it.

What it is for

Live video needs a steady connection between each camera and this device.

Step by step

  1. Put this device and the cameras on the same network. Ethernet is best for the TV; on Wi-Fi use 5 GHz if you can.
  2. Allow about 1–2 Mbit/s for each sub stream on the wall and 4–8 Mbit/s for a 1080p main stream in full screen.
  3. Open Settings > Network diagnostics and choose Check cameras: it tests whether each camera's port answers and how fast.
  4. The same screen shows this device's address, its video decoders and how many streams it plays smoothly.

What you should see

Each camera shows Reachable on port 554 (or its own port) with a time in milliseconds.

Common problems

No answer in 3 s.The camera is off, unplugged, or on another network. Check its address in its own app or your router.
Refused or unreachable.Something answered at that address but not on the RTSP port. Check the port number in the camera's settings.

20.Troubleshooting

What each message on a tile means and what to do.

What it is for

Every problem is shown in words on the tile and in Diagnostics, with a fix. One camera failing never stops the others.

Step by step

  1. CONNECTING: the app is reaching the camera. Allow up to 15 seconds.
  2. RECONNECTING: the camera dropped. The app tries again after 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and then every 60 seconds, and at once when the network comes back.
  3. OFFLINE with Wrong user name or password: edit the camera's login.
  4. The camera stopped answering: try Use TCP only, or the sub stream.
  5. This device can't play the camera's video format: see chapter 21.
  6. Use Reconnect on the wall bar to make every camera try again at once.

What you should see

When the camera is back, its tile returns to LIVE by itself.

Common problems

All cameras are offline at once.The device lost its network, or the router restarted. The wall reconnects by itself when the network is back.
The picture is delayed by a few seconds.The app keeps the buffer short for live video; a delay of about a second is normal for RTSP. Wi-Fi congestion adds more. Ethernet and sub streams help.

21.Unsupported camera formats

H.264, H.265 and MJPEG: what plays where.

What it is for

Cameras send video in different formats. Whether a format plays depends on this device's video decoder, not only on the app.

Step by step

  1. H.264 plays on every Fire TV and Fire tablet. It is the safest choice.
  2. H.265 (HEVC) plays only where the device has an H.265 decoder. Settings > Network diagnostics shows whether this one does.
  3. If a camera shows This device can't play the camera's video format, open the camera's own app or web page and set its video encoding to H.264 (for both the main and the sub stream).
  4. Some cameras offer H.264+ or H.265+ "smart codec" options; turn them off if the picture stutters.

What you should see

After switching the camera to H.264, the test shows H.264 and the picture.

Common problems

MJPEG-only cameras.Very old cameras that only send MJPEG over HTTP are not supported. Cameras that send MJPEG over RTSP may play, depending on the device.
The camera works in VLC on my computer but not here.Computers decode formats in software; TVs and tablets use hardware decoders with fewer formats. Choose H.264 on the camera.

22.Security and privacy

What the app stores, sends and never does.

What it is for

A security camera app must itself be secure. CCTV Wall Pro is built to keep your video and logins on your network and your device.

Step by step

  1. No account and no cloud: video goes straight from each camera to this device.
  2. Camera logins are encrypted with the Android Keystore, kept out of backups, logs and problem reports, and erased from the database when you delete a camera.
  3. The app connects only to the cameras you add. The ONVIF search only asks cameras on your local network to say hello.
  4. Add from your phone runs a small page on your network only while that screen is open, at a secret address in the QR code.
  5. No ads, no tracking, no analytics. Links to the Amazon Appstore and e-mail open only when you choose them.
  6. Settings > Delete all cameras and logins wipes everything.

What you should see

Camera details shows Login: Stored encrypted on this device.

Common problems

Is the camera video recorded?No. The app shows live video only and stores no video or pictures.

23.Frequently asked questions

Short answers to common questions.

What it is for

Quick answers before you contact support.

Common problems

Does it record?No, CCTV Wall Pro is a live viewer. Keep recording on your NVR, DVR or the camera's memory card.
Does it work with my NVR or DVR?Yes, if it offers RTSP or ONVIF. Add each channel as a camera; for Hikvision channel 2 is 201/202, for Dahua change channel=1 to the channel number.
How many cameras can I add?As many as you like. The wall shows up to nine at once; groups and layouts organise the rest.
Does it keep the TV awake?Yes, while the wall or a camera is showing, so the screen saver does not cover your cameras. Turn this off in Settings. The picture shifts by a few pixels each minute to protect OLED and plasma screens.
Does it need the internet?No. It needs your local network only. The online guide and Appstore links use the internet when you open them.
Will it work on Fire TV models with Vega OS?No. It is an Android app for Fire OS (Fire TV Stick, Cube, Fire TV smart TVs and Fire tablets) and Android devices.
